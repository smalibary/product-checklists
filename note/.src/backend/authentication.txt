title: Authentication (server)
about: How the server checks who someone is: passwords, codes, passkeys and sign-in limits.
## Passwords
- At least 8 characters (15 when it is the only factor), up to at least 64, all characters allowed | S nist-63b
- Checked against lists of leaked and common passwords | S nist-63b
- No forced rules about symbols and no forced regular changes | S nist-63b
- Stored with a slow, salted hash (Argon2id, scrypt or bcrypt) | S owasp-passwords
## Other ways in
- Passkeys offered where supported | S webauthn passkeys-dev
- A second factor available, required for admins | S owasp-mfa nist-63b-auth
- One-time codes expire quickly, work once and are limited in tries | S rfc-totp owasp-mfa
- Sign in with Apple or Google through standard OAuth with PKCE | S rfc-oauth-native rfc-pkce
## Attacks
- Attempts limited per account and per network address, with growing delays | S owasp-auth
- The same answer whether or not the account exists | S owasp-auth
- Password reset links random, single-use and short-lived | S owasp-forgot >flows/resetting-password
## Records
- Sign-ins, failures, resets and factor changes logged without the secrets | S owasp-logging >operations/logging >operations/secrets
- The user told by email about a new device or a changed password | S owasp-auth
## Checks
- The design checked against OWASP ASVS level 2 | S owasp-asvs >code/secure-coding
