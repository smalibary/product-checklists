title: Security page
about: The public page that tells customers, buyers and researchers how the company protects their data and how to report a weakness.
also: design-system/accessibility
## Trust summary at the top
- One plain sentence that says what is protected and how, before any detail | P
- Certifications held (SOC 2, ISO 27001, PCI DSS) shown with the year and scope, never a badge for one not held | L ftc-dark-patterns >design-system/badge
- A link to request the full audit report (often under a confidentiality agreement) | P
- A date showing when the page was last reviewed | P
- A link to the live status page | P >website/status
## Data protection
- Where data is stored (country or region) and which cloud providers are used | L gdpr-13 pdpl
- Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2 or newer) stated | S owasp-session
- Encryption at rest stated, with who holds the keys | S gdpr-32
- How long data is kept and how it is deleted when an account closes | L gdpr-13 gdpr-17
- Backups: how often, where, and how often restores are tested | P
- A list of sub-processors (third parties that handle customer data), with a way to be told of changes | L gdpr-13
## Access and accounts
- Customer-side controls listed: two-step sign-in, single sign-on, roles, session timeout | S owasp-auth owasp-mfa >web-app/2fa >backend/sessions
- Passkeys or other phishing-resistant sign-in offered or planned | S nist-63b-auth webauthn
- Staff access to customer data: least privilege, logged, reviewed | S owasp-authz gdpr-32 >operations/logging
- How staff are vetted and trained in security | P
## Application and infrastructure
- How the software is tested: code review, automatic scans, outside penetration tests and how often | S owasp-masvs >code/code-review
- Network protection named in plain terms (firewalls, DDoS protection) without giving attackers a map | P
- Logging and monitoring of suspicious activity | S owasp-logging >operations/logging
- Secrets (keys, passwords) kept in a managed vault, never in code | S owasp-secrets >operations/secrets
## Incidents
- How customers are told about a breach, and how fast | L gdpr-33
- A short incident-response outline: detect, contain, inform, review | P >operations/incident-response
- Past incidents or a link to post-incident reports, if the company publishes them | P >operations/incident-response
## Reporting a weakness
- A clear way to report a vulnerability: an email or form, what to include, and what happens next | S owasp-disclosure
- A security.txt file at /.well-known/security.txt with contact, expiry and policy link | S rfc-securitytxt
- A safe-harbour statement: good-faith researchers will not be pursued | S owasp-disclosure
- Response time promise (for example, first reply within 3 working days) | P
- Bug bounty terms and rewards, if one exists, with what is out of scope | P
- A PGP key or other way to send sensitive reports safely | S rfc-securitytxt
## Compliance and legal
- Links to the privacy policy, data-processing agreement and terms | L gdpr-13 >website/privacy
- Laws the company follows named by region (GDPR, Saudi PDPL, CCPA) | L gdpr-13 pdpl ccpa >backend/data-privacy
- Accessibility of the page itself: headings, contrast, readable without images | S wcag-headings wcag-contrast >design-system/accessibility
## Writing and layout
- Written for a buyer, not an engineer: short sections, a jump list at the top | R nng-scanning
- No vague claims like "bank-level security" or "military-grade" | R nng-trust
- A contact for security questions from buyers (security questionnaires) | P
