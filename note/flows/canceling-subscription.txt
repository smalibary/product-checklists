CANCELING SUBSCRIPTION (FLOW) CHECKLIST: 15 items
Stopping a paid plan, from finding the option to confirmation.
Backing: 10 Rule, 3 Standard, 1 Research, 1 Practice
Rule = a store or a law requires it · Standard = a published standard · Research = usability studies · Practice = good practice, no study found

FINDING IT
 1. "Cancel subscription" easy to find in billing or account settings   (Rule: ftc-negative-option)  -> see web-app/billing
 2. Cancel online if you could sign up online; no phone call required   (Rule: ftc-negative-option)  -> see website/sign-up
 3. In store-bought plans, link to the store's subscription page   (Standard: apple-subscriptions, play-subscriptions)

THE STEPS
 4. As few steps as signing up   (Rule: ftc-negative-option)
 5. One optional offer (pause, cheaper plan, discount) is fine; repeated offers or guilt wording are not   (Rule: ftc-dark-patterns)
 6. Optional short "why are you leaving" with skip   (Research: nng-surveys)
 7. Buttons say what they do ("Cancel subscription", "Keep my plan"), neither hidden   (Rule: ftc-dark-patterns)

WHAT HAPPENS
 8. Access until the end of the paid period, with the date   (Standard: apple-subscriptions)
 9. What is lost and whether data is kept, and for how long   (Rule: gdpr-13)
10. Refund rules if any   (Rule: eu-consumer-rights)  -> see flows/requesting-a-refund

CONFIRMATION
11. Confirmation screen and email with the end date   (Rule: ftc-negative-option)
12. Easy way to come back (resubscribe)   (Practice)

RULES NOTE
13. The FTC's 2024 click-to-cancel rule was struck down by a US court in 2025; the older negative-option rules and state laws still apply   (Rule: ftc-negative-option)
14. EU and UK consumer rules also require easy cancelling   (Rule: eu-consumer-rights)

ACCESS
15. No timed offers that pressure   (Standard: wcag-timing)
